# Business German Learning Plan

> **Working assumption:** this is a practical, non-exam goal with about **4 hours per week**. The first cycle runs for 24 weeks and prioritizes recurring workplace situations over broad textbook coverage.

## Definition of done

By the end of the cycle, comfortably:

- introduce your role, background, projects, and current priorities;
- participate in a 30-minute meeting, ask for clarification, and summarize decisions;
- write a clear email, follow-up, status update, and polite disagreement;
- give a 5–7 minute presentation and answer predictable questions;
- handle a short networking or interview conversation without switching to English.

## Weekly rhythm

| Session | Time | Work |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 2 input sessions | 35 min each | Read or listen to workplace German; mine useful phrases, not isolated words. |
| 2 output sessions | 35 min each | Write an email/status note and record a spoken response. |
| 1 conversation | 45–60 min | Role-play the month’s workplace scenario with correction. |
| 1 review | 25 min | Reuse corrected phrases and update the mistake log. |

Build a **phrase bank by function**: opening, clarifying, agreeing, disagreeing, proposing, delaying, summarizing, and closing.

## 24-week roadmap

### Month 1 — Professional identity

- Introductions, responsibilities, team structure, and career history.
- Core output: a natural 90-second introduction and a written professional profile.
- Grammar repair: word order, case after common prepositions, and past narration.

### Month 2 — Email and messaging

- Requests, scheduling, follow-ups, attachments, deadlines, and tone.
- Core output: five reusable email patterns, each adapted to a real situation.
- Focus on concise sentences and polite modal forms rather than ornate formality.

### Month 3 — Meetings

- Agenda language, turn-taking, clarification, opinion, disagreement, and decisions.
- Core output: lead a 15-minute simulated meeting and send the summary afterward.
- Listen for stance and action items, then paraphrase instead of translating.

### Month 4 — Projects and problem-solving

- Status, dependencies, risk, delay, trade-offs, data, and recommendations.
- Core output: a weekly two-minute project update with one risk and one request.
- Practice explaining charts and numbers without scripting every sentence.

### Month 5 — Presentations and persuasion

- Signposting, comparison, emphasis, evidence, questions, and objections.
- Core output: a recorded 5–7 minute presentation about one portfolio project.
- Repeat the presentation after feedback until delivery is stable, not memorized.

### Month 6 — Integration

- Networking, interviews, negotiation, and cross-cultural workplace scenarios.
- Core output: one 30-minute simulation that moves from small talk to a project discussion and follow-up email.
- Identify the next cycle from actual breakdowns, not a generic level checklist.

## Monthly feedback loop

At the end of each month, keep four artifacts:

1. one corrected email;
2. one recorded speaking task;
3. a list of five recurring mistakes;
4. ten high-value phrases reused in more than one situation.

Track **successful communication**, not only correctness: Did the other person understand the request, decision, risk, and next action?

## First seven days

- [ ] Record a two-minute professional introduction without notes.
- [ ] Write one realistic work email and get it corrected.
- [ ] Create the function-based phrase bank and mistake log.
- [ ] Book a weekly conversation or tutor slot.
- [ ] Choose one real project as the subject for the six-month speaking portfolio.
