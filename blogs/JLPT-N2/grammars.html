Japanese Grammar Foundations ​

These basic patterns are useful to review before studying N2 grammar. In the patterns below, N means a noun, and A/B stand for words or phrases in the sentence.

A は B です ​

Rule: は (pronounced wa) marks the topic: “as for A.” です makes a noun or な-adjective predicate polite. The topic is not always the grammatical subject.

  • 私は留学生です。 — I am an international student.
  • あの方は先生です。 — That person is a teacher.

Negative: A は B ではありません (or the conversational B じゃありません).

  • 私は先生ではありません。 — I am not a teacher.

Question: Add か to the end of a polite sentence. In writing, a question mark is optional.

  • あの方は先生ですか。 — Is that person a teacher?

Connecting predicates: B で connects a noun or な-adjective predicate to another predicate. This で is a connective form of the copula, not the particle for the location of an action.

  • 田中さんは先生で、作家です。 — Tanaka is a teacher and a writer.

Demonstratives ​

Rule: The first syllable shows where the referent is relative to the speakers: こ near the speaker, そ near the listener or something already mentioned, あ away from both, and ど for a question. In conversation, そ and あ can also refer to familiar or remembered things; physical distance is only the starting point.

PositionThingPlaceDirection / polite person
Near speakerこれこここちら
Near listenerそれそこそちら
Away from bothあれあそこあちら
Questionどれどこどちら
  • これは私の本です。 — This is my book.
  • 駅はどちらですか。 — Which way is the station? (polite)

こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら can also politely refer to a person or choice, depending on context.

N1 に N2 が あります／います ​

Rule: This pattern introduces N2 as existing at N1. に marks the location; が marks what exists. Use あります for things and plants, and います for people and animals.

  • 机の上に本があります。 — There is a book on the desk.
  • 教室に学生がいます。 — There are students in the classroom.

For possession, the location can be a person: 私には兄がいます。 — I have an older brother (literally, “As for me, an older brother exists”).

N1 には N2 が あります／います ​

Rule: には combines the location marker に with the topic or contrast marker は. It makes N1 the frame of the statement and can imply a contrast with other places or people. Use があります／います according to N2.

  • この町には大きな公園があります。 — This town has a large park.
  • 私のクラスには留学生がいます。 — There are international students in my class.

Compare 教室に学生がいます (“There are students in the classroom”) with 教室には学生がいます (“In the classroom, there are students”), which puts more focus on the classroom.

N1 は N2 に あります／います ​

Rule: Use this order when N1 is already known and you want to say where it is. は marks the known topic; に marks its location. Choose あります／います according to N1.

  • 本は机の上にあります。 — The book is on the desk.
  • 先生は職員室にいます。 — The teacher is in the staff room.

Compare 机の上に本があります (“There is a book on the desk”), which introduces the book, with 本は机の上にあります (“The book is on the desk”), which gives the location of a known book.

Past tense: noun predicates ​

Rule: For a polite noun sentence, change です to でした. The polite negative past is ではありませんでした (or conversational じゃありませんでした). These forms also work with な-adjective predicates.

MeaningPatternExample
Past affirmativeA は B でした昨日は休みでした。 — Yesterday was a day off.
Past negativeA は B ではありませんでした昨日は休みではありませんでした。 — Yesterday was not a day off.

The plain forms are だった and ではなかった (or じゃなかった): 昨日は休みだった。 — Yesterday was a day off.

Past tense: verb predicates ​

Rule: A polite verb changes from ～ます to ～ました in the affirmative past, and from ～ません to ～ませんでした in the negative past. The plain past form depends on the verb group, so it cannot be made by simply replacing ます with た.

MeaningPolitePlain
Past affirmative昨日、映画を見ました。 — I watched a film yesterday.昨日、映画を見た。
Past negative昨日、映画を見ませんでした。 — I did not watch a film yesterday.昨日、映画を見なかった。

For example, 行きます → 行きました → 行った, while 食べます → 食べました → 食べた.

Question words with が ​

Rule: When a question word asks who or what the subject is, mark it with が: 誰が／何が. The answer commonly uses が to identify that subject. Use は instead if the answer treats the person or thing as an established topic or makes a contrast; “a が question must be answered with が” is too absolute.

  • 誰が来ましたか。 — Who came?
  • 田中さんが来ました。 — Tanaka came.
  • 何がありますか。 — What is there?
  • 机の上に本があります。 — There is a book on the desk.

この／その／あの／どの ​

Rule: These are 連体詞 (words that modify a noun directly). They must be followed by a noun: この本 (“this book”), その本 (“that book near you / the book mentioned”), あの本 (“that book over there”), どの本 (“which book”). To refer to a thing without naming the noun, use これ／それ／あれ／どれ instead.

  • この本は面白いです。 — This book is interesting.
  • これは面白いです。 — This is interesting.
  • どの本があなたのですか。 — Which book is yours?
  • どれがあなたのですか。 — Which one is yours?

N を / N をください ​

Rule: The particle を follows a noun and marks the direct object. Japanese is an SOV language, so the verb usually comes at the end of the sentence; the object appears earlier and needs を to mark it.

N をください: ください is the imperative of くださる (“to give”). N をください means “please give me …” and is common when shopping or ordering.

  • 水族館の入場券をください。 — Please give me an aquarium admission ticket.
  • チーズバーガーセットをください。 — Please give me a cheeseburger set.
  • ビールをください。 — Please give me a beer.

も: To say “also / too,” use も. も and を do not stack — も replaces を.

  • それから、ホットコーヒーもください。 — And then, please give me a hot coffee too.

N を (quantity) ください ​

Rule: To say how many you want, put the quantifier (数量詞) before ください: N を [quantifier] ください. Quantifiers generally sit before the verb in Japanese.

  • この水族館の入場券を3枚ください。 — Please give me three admission tickets for this aquarium. / はい、3000円です。 — Sure, that’ll be 3000 yen.
  • チーズバーガーセットを一つください。 — Please give me one cheeseburger set.
  • あのう、これを2本ください。 — Um, please give me two of these.
  • シャツを3枚ください。 — Please give me three shirts.

Quantifiers also come before other verbs: 兄が2人います。 — I have two older brothers. / スマホが2台あります。 — There are two smartphones.

Counters used here: 枚 (flat objects), 一つ (general), 本 (long/cylindrical), 人 (people), 台 (machines).

N をお願いします / N でお願いします ​

Rule: お願いします is a polite request (“please”). It can replace ください in many ordering contexts.

  • ホットコーヒーをお願いします。 — A hot coffee, please.
  • これを４つお願いします。 — Four of these, please.

N でお願いします: The particle で marks the means / method / chosen option. Use it when specifying size, payment, channel, and so on — especially when the item itself is already clear in context.

  • Sサイズでお願いします。 — Small, please. (natural when the clerk already asked about size)
  • Sサイズのコーヒーをお願いします。 — A small coffee, please. (names the item)
  • アリペイでお願いします。 — Alipay, please. (at payment)
  • Eメールでお願いします。 — By email, please. (when asking how to contact)

N でございます ​

Rule: でございます is a more polite / formal equivalent of です (丁寧語). It is common in service speech, speeches, and other formal settings. It is typically used for the speaker, the speaker’s in-group, or things belonging to them — not usually for the respected listener or the listener’s things.

  • エレベーターはこちらでございます。 — The elevator is this way.
  • 私が担当者でございます。 — I am the person in charge.
  • 部長の田中でございます。 — This is Department Manager Tanaka. (colleague introducing their own company’s manager to a client)

Dialogue:

  • A (outsider): 田中部長は、今日、お休みですか。 — Is Manager Tanaka off today?
  • B (colleague): はい、休みでございます。 — Yes, he is off today.

どうなさいますか / ～はどうですか ​

Rule: どう means “how / what (choice).” なさいます is the honorific form of する, so どうなさいますか is a very polite “What would you like?” / “How would you like it?” — common in service speech.

  • A: ホットコーヒーのサイズはどうなさいますか。 — What size hot coffee would you like?
  • B: Lサイズでお願いします。 — Large, please.

～はどうですか: When どう is the predicate with ですか, ～はどうですか asks for an opinion or how something is going.

  • このコーヒーはどうですか。 — How is this coffee?
  • 清華大学はどうですか。 — How is Tsinghua University?
  • 最近はどうですか。 — How have you been lately?

Adjective predicates (polite non-past) ​

Rule: Adjectival predicates describe a state or a constant property. Japanese adjectives fall into two types: Type I (い-adjectives, ending in い) and Type II (な-adjectives / adjectival verbs).

Polite non-past (敬体):

  1. Affirmative: Type I → A₁ + です; Type II → A₂ + です.
  2. Negative: Type I → drop い, then く + ありません; Type II → A₂ + ではありません (spoken: じゃありません).
CategoryTenseAffirmative (polite)Negative (polite)
Type I (e.g. 高い)Non-past高いです高くありません
Type II (e.g. 静か)Non-past静かです静かではありません（じゃありません）

では can be replaced by じゃ in spoken Japanese.

  • 東京は寒いです。 — Tokyo is cold.
  • あの映画は面白くありません。 — That movie is not interesting.
  • 先生は親切です。 — The teacher is kind.
  • 日本語は簡単ではありません。 — Japanese is not easy.

いい: Conjugate from the older form よい. Negative of いいです → よくありません.

  • 成績はよくありません。 — The grades are not good.

N₁ は N₂ が 〜 ​

Rule: は marks the topic (what the sentence is about); が marks the subject of the predicate that follows. In A は B が X です, A is usually a person or a whole thing, B is a part or aspect of A, and B が X です describes a property of A. This “topic + part/aspect” pattern is common in Japanese and often differs from how Chinese or English packages the same idea.

  • 象は鼻が長いです。 — Elephants have long trunks. / An elephant’s trunk is long.
  • 富士山は形がいいです。 — Mt. Fuji has a nice shape.
  • 今日は天気がいいです。 — The weather is good today.
  • 彼女は性格が明るいです。 — She has a cheerful personality.

あまり / そんなに + adjective (negative) ​

Rule: The degree adverbs あまり and そんなに with a negative adjective mean “not very …” / “not that …”. Use them with the polite negative forms of Type I and Type II adjectives (e.g. 寒くありません, 難しくありません).

  • A: 東京は寒いですか。 — Is Tokyo cold?
  • B: いいえ、あまり寒くありません。 — No, it’s not very cold.
  • 日本語はそんなに難しくありません。 — Japanese is not that difficult.
  • このパソコンはそんなに遅くありません。 — This computer is not that slow.

〜から（reason） ​

Rule: から at the end of a clause or sentence marks a reason / cause (“because …”). It can stand alone as an answer or follow a statement that gives the conclusion first.

  • （中村さんは）とても真面目ですから。 — Because (Nakamura-san) is very serious / diligent.
  • アイスクリームがいいです。暑いですから。 — Ice cream would be good. Because it’s hot.
  • A: 寒いですね。 — It’s cold, isn’t it?
  • B: そうですね。今日は雪ですから。 — Yes. Because it’s snowing today.

N はどうですか（asking about nature / opinion） ​

Rule: どう means “how / what is it like.” N はどうですか asks about the nature of something or the listener’s opinion of it. Answer with an adjectival predicate (or a short evaluative phrase).

  • A: 日本の留学生生活はどうですか。 — How is life as an international student in Japan?
  • B: 順調ですよ。 — It’s going well.
  • A: 日本語はどうですか。 — How is Japanese?
  • B: 難しいです。 — It’s difficult.
  • A: この映画はどうですか。 — How is this movie?
  • B: とても面白いです。 — It’s very interesting.

〜よ（assertion / emphasis） ​

Rule: Sentence-final よ marks the speaker’s assertion, strong claim, or emphatic tone — often new information or a firm reminder for the listener.

  • 中村さんのおかげですよ。 — It’s thanks to Nakamura-san.
  • 山羊座の人はみんな真面目ですよ。 — Capricorns are all serious / diligent.
  • 日本語はそんなに難しくありませんよ。 — Japanese isn’t that difficult.

〜ね（soft exclamation） ​

Rule: Sentence-final ね can mark a light exclamation / shared impression. Read it with a falling tone (↘). For a softer stretch, lengthen it to ねえ.

  • 由加ちゃんはすごいですね。↘ — Yuka-chan is really amazing.
  • この洋服、かわいいですね。↘ — These clothes are cute.
  • 由加ちゃんのこのスマホケース、かわいいですね。↘ — Yuka-chan, this phone case of yours is cute.

Adjective predicates (polite past) ​

Rule: Past forms of adjectival predicates differ by type.

  1. Past affirmative: Type I → drop い, then かったです; Type II → A₂ + でした.
  2. Past negative: Type I → drop い, then く + ありませんでした; Type II → A₂ + ではありませんでした (spoken: じゃありませんでした).
CategoryTenseAffirmative (polite)Negative (polite)
Type I (e.g. 高い)Non-past高いです高くありません
Past高かったです高くありませんでした
Type II (e.g. 静か)Non-past静かです静かではありません（じゃありません）
Past静かでした静かではありませんでした（じゃありませんでした）

では can be replaced by じゃ in spoken Japanese.

  • A: お客さんは多かったですか。 — Were there many customers?
  • B: ううん、平日の昼間ですから、ばらばらでしたよ。 — No — it was a weekday afternoon, so they were sparse.
  • 子供の時、生活は豊かではありませんでした。 — When I was a child, life was not affluent.
  • 10年前の家賃はそんなに高くありませんでした。 — Ten years ago, the rent wasn’t that high.

ない（negation） / adjective–noun quadrants ​

Rule: ない means “not / does not exist.” It is itself a Type I adjective, and it also works as an auxiliary adjective after other adjectives (and in noun/Type II patterns) to mark negation.

Adding です after ない is more polite and pairs with the ありません forms:

  • Non-past: ありません ＝ ないです
  • Past: ありませんでした ＝ なかったです

Together, tense (non-past / past) × polarity (affirmative / negative) make the adjective / noun quadrants (四象限).

Adjective quadrant (polite) ​

CategoryTenseAffirmativeNegative
Type I (e.g. 高い)Non-past高いです高くありません ／ 高くないです
Past高かったです高くありませんでした ／ 高くなかったです
Type II (e.g. 静か)Non-past静かです静かではありません（じゃありません） ／ 静かではないです（じゃないです）
Past静かでした静かではありませんでした（じゃありませんでした） ／ 静かではなかったです（じゃなかったです）

Noun quadrant (polite) ​

ClassTenseAffirmativeNegative
Noun (e.g. 学生)Non-past学生です学生ではありません（じゃありません） ／ 学生ではないです（じゃないです）
Past学生でした学生ではありませんでした（じゃありませんでした） ／ 学生ではなかったです（じゃなかったです）

では can be replaced by じゃ in spoken Japanese.

Connecting adjectives ​

Rule: To link adjectives in a parallel / sequential chain, use the continuative form of the preceding adjective. The form depends only on that adjective, not on what follows. Tense is carried by the final predicate; the middle form does not mark tense.

  • Type I: drop い → くて (A₁ い → く + て)

  • Type II: A₂ + で

  • 映画の主人公は背が高くて、魅力的です。 — The movie’s protagonist is tall and charming. (高くて = Type I; 魅力的 = Type II)

  • 昨日の試験問題は複雑で、難しかったです。 — Yesterday’s exam questions were complicated and difficult. (複雑で = Type II; final past on 難しい)

  • 映画の主人公は背が高くて、かっこいいです。 — …tall and cool. (Type I + Type I — still 高くて)

  • 映画の主人公は背が高くて、魅力的です。 — …tall and charming. (Type I + Type II — still 高くて)

  • 昨日の映画の主人公は背が高くて、魅力的でした。 — Yesterday’s protagonist was tall and charming. (middle stays 高くて; past only on でした)

N まで（endpoint） ​

Rule: まで follows a noun and marks an endpoint / limit (“until / up to / as far as”). It works with time nouns (this lesson) and with place nouns for a spatial endpoint. N まで can also take です as a noun-predicate sentence.

  • わたしは昨日、遅くまで日本語の試験でした。 — Yesterday my Japanese exam went until late.
  • 今日の授業はここまでです。 — Today’s class ends here. / That’s as far as today’s lesson goes.
  • あの歌手のライブツアーは来月20日までです。 — That singer’s live tour runs until the 20th of next month.

全然 + adjective (negative) ​

Rule: Like あまり, 全然 is a degree adverb that goes with a negative adjective: “not … at all” / “completely not …”. In spoken Japanese, 全然 alone can answer “not at all.”

  • A: 昨日の試験は難しかったですか。 — Was yesterday’s exam difficult?
  • B: 全然難しくなかったですよ。 — It wasn’t difficult at all.
  • わたしは運動が全然好きではありません。 — I don’t like exercise at all.
  • 彼女の料理は全然美味しくありません。 — Her cooking isn’t delicious at all.
  • A: 日本語の試験は難しかったですか。 — Was the Japanese exam difficult?
  • B: ううん、全然。 — No, not at all.

N とか（listing examples） ​

Rule: とか attaches to nouns to list examples (things like …). Common in spoken Japanese; it can appear once or several times. With とか, topic/subject/object particles は / が / を can be omitted.

  • A: どんな飲み物が好きですか。 — What kind of drinks do you like?
  • B: ミルクティーとか、コーラとか。 — Milk tea, cola, that sort of thing.
  • 木曜日の午後以降とかはどうですか。 — How about something like Thursday afternoon onward?

Adjectives modifying nouns (attributive form / 连体形) ​

Rule: Besides acting as predicates, adjectives can sit before a noun and modify it. That form is the attributive form (连体形).

  • Type I (い-adjectives): A₁ + noun — the adjective attaches to the noun directly (keeps its い ending).

  • Type II (な-adjectives): A₂ + な + noun — insert な before the noun. Some books call these な-adjectives for that reason.

  • 白いパソコン — white computer (Type I: 白い + パソコン)

  • きれいな町 — clean / beautiful town (Type II: きれい + な + 町)

N が（subject）— は vs が as subject markers ​

IMPORTANT

Easy to confuse for a long time: both は and が can mark what looks like a “subject,” but they answer different information questions. が = the subject is new / unknown to the listener (focus on the noun before が). は = the subject is already known (old information); focus falls on what comes after は.

Rule: When introducing or identifying something the listener does not yet have as given, use が. Once that referent is established in the conversation, switch to は. Wh-questions about the subject (だれが, 何が, …) take が; the answer usually keeps が on the identified item, then later sentences about that person/thing use は.

  • ほら、あそこの男の人。あの人は中村さんのお兄さんですよ。 — Look, that man over there. He is Nakamura’s older brother. (あの人 is already pointed out → は; focus on who he is.)
  • A: へえ、小学校の時の記念写真ですか。中村さんは、これ？ — Oh, an elementary-school commemorative photo? Nakamura, is this you?
  • B: 違いますよ。これがわたしです。 — No. This is me. (identifying which one → が; focus on これ.)
  • こちらのパソコンが、今お得ですよ。 — This PC is a good deal right now. (picking out this one as new focus → が.)
  • A: 日本語は難しいですか。 — Is Japanese difficult? (topic = Japanese → は.)
  • B: ええ、文法が難しいです。 — Yes — the grammar is what’s difficult. (specifying the hard part as new focus → が.)
  • A: だれが留学生ですか。 — Who is the international student? (だれが — always が.)
  • B: あの人が留学生で、ジョージさんです。ジョージさんは今、先生の研究室にいます。 — That person is the international student — George. George is in the teacher’s lab now. (identify with が, then known George → は.)

N と同じ（same as N） ​

Rule: 同じ is a Type II adjective meaning “the same / identical.” Attach と after the noun you compare with: N と同じ = “the same as N.”

Exception (easy miss): When 同じ modifies a noun attributively, do not add な — write 同じ + noun directly (not 同じな).

  • 白いパソコンと同じ大きさです。 — It’s the same size as the white computer.
  • 李さんは中村さんと同じ大学です。 — Li is at the same university as Nakamura.
  • 経済学部は法学部と同じ建物です。 — The economics department is in the same building as the law department.

N がほしいです（want N） ​

Rule: ほしい is a Type I adjective meaning “want (to obtain something).” Conjugate it like other Type I adjectives. The thing wanted is usually marked with が: N がほしいです ≈ “I want N.”

Person restriction: Use ほしい mainly for the speaker’s desire (first person). Avoid it for third-person wants; for the listener, it is mostly OK with close family/friends.

ほしい can also modify a noun attributively (ほしい + noun).

  • （私は）新しいスーツケースがほしいです。 — I want a new suitcase.
  • 地下鉄がなかった時は、車がほしかったです。 — When there was no subway, I wanted a car.
  • A: ほしいものはありますか。 — Is there anything you want?
  • B: ありますよ。お金がほしいです。 — Yes. I want money.

Clause₁ が、Clause₂（adversative / softener） ​

Rule: Mid-sentence が can link two clauses with a contrast / turning sense (“…, but …” / “although Clause₁, Clause₂”). Soften the tone after set phrases like すみませんが / 失礼ですが — the adversative feel is weak and often need not be translated as “but.” Clause₂ can also be omitted, leaving a trailing が that softens a request or wish.

  • 歴史の授業は難しいですが、面白いです。 — History class is difficult, but interesting.
  • 失礼ですが、お名前は？ — Excuse me — what’s your name?
  • 新しいパソコンがほしいのですが。 — I’d like a new computer… (Clause₂ left open / soft request.)

N₁ も N₂ も（both … and …） ​

Rule: Put も after each of two nouns in parallel: N₁ も N₂ も ～. It lists both items as sharing the same predicate — “both N₁ and N₂ …” / “N₁ and N₂ alike …”.

  • 兄も姉も会社員です。 — Both my older brother and older sister are company employees.
  • 日本料理も中華料理も美味しいです。 — Both Japanese food and Chinese food are delicious.
  • メモリーもストレージも大きいです。 — Both the memory and the storage are large.

Comparison patterns（比較） ​

Japanese has several fixed frames for comparing two nouns. The four below are the core set from this lesson.

(1) N₁ は N₂ より ～ ​

Rule: より marks the baseline of comparison. N₁ は N₂ より ～ means N₁ has more of the predicate property than N₂ (“N₁ is …-er than N₂”). When the gap is large, add ずっと (“much more …”).

  • 中国は日本より広いです。 — China is larger than Japan.
  • （こちらのパソコンは）さっきのよりメモリーがずっと大きいですよ。 — (This PC’s) memory is much larger than the one just now.
  • スマホはパソコンより値段が高いです。 — Smartphones cost more than computers.

(2) N₂ より N₁ のほうが ～ ​

Rule: Same comparison, different packaging: N₂ より N₁ のほうが ～ = “compared with N₂, N₁ is the one that is …”. ほう (方) means “side / one of the two.” Often used to answer pattern (4); in answers, N₂ より is frequently dropped → N₁ のほうが ～.

  • 日本より中国のほうが広いです。 — Compared with Japan, China is larger.
  • パソコンよりスマホのほうが値段が高いです。 — Compared with PCs, smartphones are more expensive.

(3) N₂ は N₁ ほど ～（negative） ​

Rule: ほど after a noun marks degree. With a negative predicate, N₂ は N₁ ほど ～ない means N₂ does not reach N₁’s degree — “N₂ is not as … as N₁.”

  • 日本は中国ほど広くありません。 — Japan is not as large as China.
  • パソコンはスマホほど値段が高くありません。 — Computers are not as expensive as smartphones.

(4) N₁ と N₂ とどちら（のほう）が ～か ​

Rule: Ask which of two is more …: N₁ と N₂ とどちら（のほう）が ～か. Answer with pattern (2), usually just N のほうが ～.

  • A: 中国と日本とどちら（のほう）が広いですか。 — Between China and Japan, which is larger?
  • B: 中国のほうが広いです。 — China is larger.
  • A: パソコンとスマホとどちらが高いですか。 — Between a PC and a smartphone, which is more expensive?
  • B: スマホのほうが高いです。 — The smartphone is.

N なので、～（cause and reason II） ​

Rule: ので connects two clauses to express a cause and its result. With a noun predicate, use N + な + ので, meaning “because (it is) N, …” (see Lesson 5, Grammar 1). Compared with から (Lesson 9, Grammar 4), ので emphasizes an objective cause and sounds softer and more formal.

  • 僕は神奈川県の湘南の出身なので、僕も『スラムダンク』が大好きですよ。 — Because I’m from Shōnan in Kanagawa Prefecture, I love Slam Dunk, too.
  • 雨なので、運動会は中止です。 — Because of the rain, the sports day is canceled.
  • 学生なので、お金がないです。 — Because I’m a student, I don’t have any money.

N が～（object marker が III） ​

Rule: Besides marking the subject, the particle が can also mark an object. In this lesson, が marks the object of an ability or skill level. These expressions are mainly Type II (na-) adjectives and verbs.

  • Type II (na-) adjectives: N が 上手／下手 (good／poor at), 好き／嫌い (like／dislike), 得意／苦手 (strong／weak at) です。

  • Verbs: N が できます (can do), わかります (understand).

  • 私は運動が嫌いです。 — I don’t like exercise.

  • 辛いものが苦手です。 — I can’t handle spicy food.

  • 兄は歴史が得意です。 — My older brother is good at history.

  • 先生の話がよくわかりません。 — I don’t understand the teacher’s explanation very well.

  • 父は料理ができます。 — My father can cook.

（N₁の中で）N₂がいちばん～（superlative; で marks range） ​

Rule: Building on the comparison patterns, this is the superlative: within the range given by N₁, N₂ has the most of the quality the predicate describes. The particle で after a noun limits the range, and the adverb いちばん means "most". A superlative singles out the most outstanding person or thing among three or more. Translate as "(Among/In N₁,) N₂ is the most ～."

  • 日本の漫画の中で、『スラムダンク』がいちばん好きです。 — Among Japanese manga, I like Slam Dunk the most.
  • スポーツの中でバスケットボールがいちばん好きです。 — Among sports, I like basketball the most.
  • クラスの中で、李さんがいちばん頭がいいです。 — In the class, Li is the smartest.
  • 日本料理の中で、お寿司がいちばん好きです。 — Among Japanese dishes, I like sushi the most.

～は～が、～は～。（contrastive は II） ​

Rule: In an adversative sentence joined by が ("but"), when the two clauses have a similar shape and contrast with each other, both topics are marked with は. The は … は pair carries the contrast.

  • バスケットボールはできませんが、サッカーとテニスはすこしできます。 — I can't play basketball, but I can play a little soccer and tennis.
  • 日本語は上手ですが、英語は下手です。 — I'm good at Japanese, but bad at English.
  • 昨日は寒かったですが、今日はとても暑いです。 — It was cold yesterday, but it's very hot today.
  • 私は学生ですが、妹は会社員です。 — I'm a student, but my younger sister is a company employee.

N に～（target of a state or quality） ​

Rule: After a noun, the particle に marks the target that a following ability, attitude, or quality applies to, like English "for / about / at N". The predicate is usually an adjective of quality, state, or ability, such as 必要, 便利, or 詳しい.

  • マスクは外出に必要です。 — Masks are necessary for going out.
  • 横の中国語訳は日本語学習に便利です。 — The Chinese translation beside it is useful for studying Japanese.
  • 兄は中国の歴史にも、日本の歴史にも詳しいです。 — My older brother knows a lot about both Chinese and Japanese history.
  • 駅の近くのアパートは、買い物に便利です。 — The apartment near the station is convenient for shopping.

～の（／ん）です（explanation I: adjectives and nouns） ​

IMPORTANT

The most important pattern in this lesson. ～の（／ん）です turns a plain statement into an explanation: it presents the fact as the reason, background, or "the thing is…" behind the situation. Speech usually shortens it to ～んです. Chinese and English have no direct equivalent, so it is usually left untranslated.

Connection:

  • N + な + の（／ん）です
  • Aɪ (い-adjective) + の（／ん）です
  • Aɪɪ (な-adjective) + な + の（／ん）です

Rule: This pattern is very common in conversation. It attaches to the attributive (連体形) form of an adjective or to N + な, and in speech ～んです is preferred. It states the speaker's own view, or explains or clarifies a situation. It has three main uses.

(1) Explaining a situation or giving a reason ​

Gives the reason or cause behind something already mentioned, mostly to explain the speaker's own circumstances.

  • 実は『キャプテン翼』も『テニスの王子様』も好きなんですよ。 — Actually, I like both Captain Tsubasa and The Prince of Tennis, too.
  • すみません、今はちょっと忙しいんです。 — Sorry, I'm a bit busy right now.
  • 私は冷たい飲み物が苦手なんです。 — I don't really like cold drinks.

(2) Agreement and realization ​

What was said before becomes the reason for the のです sentence. The speaker uses it to accept or confirm the conclusion, as in "so that's how it is", often confirming their own guess.

  • 由加さんは本当に漫画ファンなのですね。 — So you really are a manga fan, Yuka.

(3) Giving background for a tactful suggestion or request ​

～の（／ん）ですが、～ sets out the background first, and what the speaker actually wants is linked to it. Often the sentence stops at が and the listener infers the request.

  • 新しいパソコンがほしいのですが。 — I'd like a new computer… (to a shop assistant)
  • マックの最新モデルがいちばん軽いのですが。 — The latest Mac is the lightest one… (would you consider it?)
  • あのう、テレビの音が大きいのですが。 — Um, the TV is rather loud… (could you turn it down?)

Notes: The first example is how you tell a shop assistant what you need. The second explains a fact while hinting "why not consider it?". The third politely describes the situation and implies the request to lower the volume.

N（place）＋へ（／に）＋movement verb（direction of movement） ​

Rule: After a noun for a place, the particle へ marks the direction of movement. It pairs with movement verbs such as 行く (go), 来る (come), and 帰る (go/come home). In this use へ is pronounced e, not he. へ can also be replaced by に.

  • 毎朝、会社へ行きます。 — I go to work every morning.
  • いつ帰りますか。 — When are you going home?
  • 去年、家族と大阪に来ました。 — I came to Osaka with my family last year.

疑問詞 ＋ も（total negation） ​

Rule: A question word + も used with a negative predicate gives total negation: "no one / nothing / none / nowhere". 誰も means no one, 何も nothing, どれも none of them, and どこにも nowhere. A particle like に goes between the question word and も, as in どこにも.

  • 夜中の博物館には誰もいません。 — There is no one in the museum in the middle of the night.
  • 引き出しには何もありません。 — There is nothing in the drawer.
  • ここのカレー料理はどれもおいしくないです。 — None of the curry dishes here taste good.
  • この週末はどこにも行きません。 — I'm not going anywhere this weekend.

疑問詞 ＋ か（indefinite: some-） ​

Rule: A question word + か makes it indefinite: someone, something, some number of. 誰か means someone, 何人か several people, いくつか a few, and 何回か a few times. It works in statements as well as questions. When 疑問詞か appears in a question, the question becomes an ordinary yes/no question, so the answer usually starts with はい or いいえ. Compare 疑問詞 + も + negative, which means "no one / nothing".

  • あのレストランにお客さんが何人かいます。 — There are a few customers in that restaurant.
  • 特に重要な理由がいくつかあります。 — There are a few especially important reasons.
  • A：あのう、すみません。誰かいますか。 — A: Um, excuse me. Is anyone there?
  • B：はい。 — B: Yes.
  • 以前、何回か来ました。 — I've been here a few times before.

N から（starting point） ​

Rule: After a noun of time, place, or order, the particle から marks the starting point, like English "from". から can be followed directly by です to make a noun-predicate sentence ("…starts from N"). The starting-point から also pairs with まで, which marks the end point: N₁ から N₂ まで, "from N₁ to N₂".

  • 体のトレーニングは明日からです。 — Physical training starts tomorrow.
  • 今から成績の発表です。 — We'll now announce the results.
  • ここからはテストの内容です。 — From here on is what's on the test.
  • 会議は今月の4日から6日までです。 — The meeting runs from the 4th to the 6th of this month.

N じゃありませんか（surprised "isn't it…?"） ​

Rule: This is the rhetorical-question form of the noun sentence N です, like "isn't it N?" or "why, it's N!". It doesn't really ask anything. It carries surprise or exclamation, and is used mainly in speech.

  • とても有名な研究所じゃありませんか。 — Why, this is a really famous research institute!
  • もう12時50分じゃありませんか。 — It's already 12:50!
  • あ、あの人は、鈴木さんじゃありませんか。 — Oh, isn't that Suzuki over there?

…けど。（sentence-final softened contrast） ​

Rule: The conjunction けど is short for けれども and marks contrast ("but"). Here it comes at the end of the sentence as a softened contrast: the speaker stops partway and leaves the second half unsaid. That makes the tone gentle and indirect, and it is used mostly in speech. Besides けど, けれども also shortens to けれど and けども.

  • これから会議なんですけど。 — We're about to start the meeting… (so, if you don't mind)
  • そろそろお時間ですけれども。 — It's about time…
  • A：中村さんはいますか。 — A: Is Nakamura here?
  • B：はい、私ですけど。 — B: Yes, that's me… (what is it?)

NOTE

Start of verbs（動詞）. Everything above covers nouns, adjectives and particles. From here on, the notes cover verbs.

Verb conjugation classes（動詞の活用分類） ​

Rule: Sorting verbs by conjugation class is the first step to using verb sentences well. Traditional Japanese school grammar and today's popular textbooks classify verbs a little differently, but mostly only the names differ. The dictionary form, also called the basic form, is the unconjugated form a verb has as a dictionary entry.

ClassJapanese nameFormExamples
Class I五段動詞Dictionary form ends in a う-row kana休む、願う、わかる、ある
Class II一段動詞Dictionary form ends in る, preceded by an い-row or え-row kana寝る、起きる、忘れる
Class IIIサ変動詞する on its own, or Sino-Japanese word + するする、勉強する、運動する、運転する
Class IIIカ変動詞Only one verb: 来る来る

V ます形（polite form and the ます-stem） ​

Rule: In use, a verb usually takes the polite ending ます after its dictionary form. Before ます is added, the verb changes according to its conjugation class (see the table above). This change is called verb conjugation（動詞の活用）.

Class I（五段動詞） ​

Change the final kana from the う-row to the matching い-row kana, then add ます.

  • 休む: む → み + ます = 休みます
  • 願う: う → い + ます = 願います
  • ある: る → り + ます = あります

Class II（一段動詞） ​

Drop the final る, then add ます.

  • 起きる: drop る + ます = 起きます
  • 寝る: drop る + ます = 寝ます
  • 忘れる: drop る + ます = 忘れます

Class III（サ変・カ変） ​

  • サ変 する: する → し + ます = します; 勉強する → 勉強し + ます = 勉強します
  • カ変 来る: 来る（くる）→ 来（き）+ ます = 来ます（きます）

The ます-stem ​

What is left after removing ます from the polite form is the ます-stem, labelled V ます形 in these notes. Japanese school grammar calls it the （第一）連用形. For example, ある has the polite form あります, so its ます-stem is あり.

Class II忘れる忘れます忘れ

Class III来る（くる）来ます（きます）来（き）

N（time）に（point in time） ​

Rule: After a time noun, the particle に marks the point in time when an action happens, like English "at / on".

  • 日曜日の午後1時半に会いましょうか。 — Shall we meet at 1:30 on Sunday afternoon?
  • いつも夜9時に寝ます。 — I always go to bed at 9 p.m.
  • 13日に中村さんの誕生日パーティーがあります。 — There's a birthday party for Nakamura on the 13th.

N（time）＋ごろ（approximate time） ​

Rule: The suffix ごろ (also written 頃) after a specific time noun means "around / about" that time. With ごろ, the time-point particle に can be dropped: both 2時ごろ帰ります and 2時ごろに帰ります work.

  • いつも夜11時ごろ寝ます。 — I always go to bed around 11 p.m.
  • 今日は2時ごろに帰ります。 — Today I'll go home around 2.
  • 3月ごろ、桜が咲きます。 — Cherry blossoms bloom around March.
  • 2000年ごろから、車が多くなりました。 — From around 2000, there have been more and more cars.

数量詞＋くらい／ぐらい（about, approximately） ​

Rule: くらい (also ぐらい) after a quantity word for time, money, or a count means "about / approximately" that amount, like "or so" in English.

TIP

Compare it with ごろ: ごろ marks an approximate point in time (2時ごろ, around 2 o'clock), while くらい marks an approximate amount or length (2時間ぐらい, about two hours).

  • 発表は全部で2時間ぐらいでした。 — The presentation took about two hours in all.
  • 食費は毎月3万円ぐらいです。 — Food costs about 30,000 yen a month.
  • 教室には学生が5人ぐらいいます。 — There are about five students in the classroom.

なる（change of state） ​

Rule: なる ("to become") describes a thing or its state changing naturally. It combines with nouns, adjectives, and verbs; this lesson covers nouns and adjectives.

Word typePatternExample
NounN ＋ に ＋ なる会社員 → 会社員になる
い-adjectivedrop い → く ＋ なる寒い → 寒くなる
な-adjectiveA ＋ に ＋ なる静か → 静かになる
  • 11月の初めから寒くなります。 — It starts getting cold from early November.
  • 体調が大分よくなりました。 — My health has gotten a lot better.
  • 日本語がかなり上手になりました。 — My Japanese has gotten quite good.
  • 今週末のイベントは中止になりました。 — This weekend's event has been called off.
  • でも、午後から眠くなるから、何もできなくなります。 — But from the afternoon on I get sleepy, so I can't do anything anymore.

Note: 何もできなくなります means "(it comes to the point that) I can't do anything." できない is the negative of できる (can, be able to). When a negative ない form is followed by なる, ない becomes なく: できない → できなくなる, a change into not being able to.

Vましょう／Vましょうか（invitation: let's / shall we） ​

Rule: ます-stem ＋ ましょう invites the listener to do something together with you, often with いっしょに: "let's …". Adding the question particle か (Vましょうか) makes it softer: you are asking whether the other person agrees.

  • そろそろ帰りましょう。 — Let's head home soon.
  • A：昼休みは、あそこの店へ行きましょうか。 — A: Shall we go to that place over there at lunch?
  • B：そうしましょう。 — B: Yes, let's do that.
  • A：今晩、家でゲームしましょうか。 — A: Shall we play games at my place tonight?
  • B：いいですね。 — B: Sounds good.

～ほうがいい（advice: it's better that…, with nouns and adjectives） ​

Rule: ほう means "side / direction". Attaching ほうがいい gives the speaker's advice to the listener: "… would be better."

Word typePatternExample
NounN ＋ の ＋ ほうがいい効率的なもの → 効率的なもののほうがいい
い-adjectivedictionary form ＋ ほうがいい近い → 近いほうがいい
な-adjectiveA ＋ な ＋ ほうがいい安全 → 安全なほうがいい
  • もっと効率的なもののほうがいいですよ。 — Something more efficient would be better.
  • 新しいアパートは会社に近いほうがいいです。 — It's better if the new apartment is close to the office.
  • 毎日の食材は安全なほうがいいです。 — Everyday ingredients had better be safe.

Transitive verbs and を（marking the direct object） ​

Rule: Japanese verbs can be intransitive or transitive. An intransitive verb does not take a direct object; a transitive verb can. Some verbs can be used in both ways. With a transitive verb, put を after the noun that is its direct object.

  • 本を読みます。 — I read a book.
  • 資料を調べます。 — I examine the materials.

When the direct object becomes the topic or is marked as also, は or も can replace を.

  • メールはよく送りますが、手紙はあまり書きません。 — I often send emails, but I do not write letters very often.
  • 休みの日は、よく映画を見ます。買い物もします。 — On my days off, I often watch movies. I also go shopping.

N で（place of an action; means or tool） ​

Place of an action ​

Rule: Put で after a place noun to show where an action takes place. In English, it often corresponds to "at" or "in".

  • 伊藤先生のゼミでデータを集めました。 — I collected data in Professor Ito's seminar.
  • 私はよく喫茶店で小説を読みます。 — I often read novels at a coffee shop.
  • 日本では、口頭試験はどんな準備をしますか。 — In Japan, how do you prepare for oral exams?

Means or tool ​

Rule: Put で after a noun to show the tool or means used to do an action. In English, it often corresponds to "with" or "by".

  • スマホで外国語を勉強します。 — I study foreign languages with my smartphone.
  • インターネットで資料を調べます。 — I look up information on the internet.
  • 日本人はお箸で料理を食べます。 — Japanese people eat food with chopsticks.

N に（recipient or target of an action） ​

Rule: Put に after a noun to mark the person an action is directed toward. Depending on the verb, this person may be the recipient of something, someone you meet, or someone you ask.

  • 中村さんにメールで資料を送ります。 — I send the materials to Nakamura by email.
  • 午後、ジョージさんに会います。 — I will meet George in the afternoon.
  • 友達に簡単なやり方を聞きます。 — I ask a friend for a simple way to do it.

N なら（bringing up a topic） ​

Rule: Put なら after a noun to bring that noun forward as the topic of what follows. The topic is often something already mentioned or something the listener can infer from the conversation. N なら、～ can mean "as for N, …" or, when responding to someone's interest or request, "if it's N you're talking about, …".

  • 日本語の小説なら、家には何冊かありますよ。 — As for Japanese novels, I have a few at home.
  • A：新しいパソコンがほしいんですが。 — I'd like a new computer.
  • B：パソコンなら、こちらがいいですよ。 — If you're looking for a computer, this one is good.
  • A：一緒に喫茶店に行きましょうか。 — Shall we go to a coffee shop together?
  • B：コーヒーなら、スタバのがいいですね。 — If we're going for coffee, Starbucks would be good.

N について（about a topic） ​

Rule: Put について after a noun to introduce the subject being discussed: "about N". It can be followed by です to form a predicate, or it can appear before a verb phrase describing what someone says, writes, or asks about that topic.

  • 経済のグローバリゼーションについてです。 — It's about economic globalization.
  • 先週の旅行について作文を書きました。 — I wrote an essay about last week's trip.
  • 今日の授業について、何か質問はありますか。 — Do you have any questions about today's class?
