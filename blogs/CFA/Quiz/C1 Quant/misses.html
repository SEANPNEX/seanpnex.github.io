2026-10-23 — Multiple regression (M1) ​

Q1: List the assumptions of the multiple regression model.

My answer: Left out independence of errors.

Correct: Linearity; homoskedasticity; independence of errors (no serial correlation); normality of errors; independence of the Xs (no perfect multicollinearity, Xs not random).

Gap: Missed the no-serial-correlation assumption.

Q2: Interpret a slope coefficient in a multiple regression.

My answer: Change in Y for a one-unit change in X_j.

Correct: Expected change in Y for a one-unit change in Xj, holding all other Xs constant, so it is a partial regression coefficient. b0 is the expected Y when all Xs are 0.

Gap: Left out "holding all other Xs constant".

2026-11-15 — Time series (M5) ​

Q: An AR(1) is estimated on the first-differenced series yt=xt−xt−1: yt=b0+b1yt−1+εt. The slope is 0.052 (t=0.585) and the intercept is insignificant (t=−1.13). What process does the original series xt follow?

My answer: Covariance stationary.

Correct: Random walk (without drift). Neither coefficient in the differenced AR(1) differs significantly from 0, consistent with b0=0 and b1=0: a first-differenced random walk is just yt=εt, so nothing is left to predict. The original series is the levels AR(1) with b0=0 and b1=1, xt=xt−1+εt. It has a unit root, so it is not covariance stationary (Var(xt)=(t−1)σ2 grows without bound) and its mean-reverting level b0/(1−b1) is undefined (0/0). The first difference is covariance stationary; that is the evidence that the original series is a random walk, which is not.

Gap: Chose covariance stationary.
