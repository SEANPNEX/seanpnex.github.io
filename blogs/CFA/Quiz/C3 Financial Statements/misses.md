# FSA / Intercorporate Investments — quiz misses

## 2026-10-14 — Debt FVOCI impairment (ECL vs AOCI vs B/S)

**Q:** Debt FVOCI: FV falls 980→960, ECL $15. What hits I/S, what hits AOCI, and what carrying amount shows on B/S?

**My answer:** ECL $15 hits I/S; also thought $15 hits the asset on B/S; RE −15; AOCI −5 (or total FV move −20).

**Correct:**
- I/S / RE: **−15** (ECL)
- AOCI: **−5** (= −20 FV drop + 15 ECL already in earnings)
- B/S asset stays at **FV 960** — ECL does **not** write the asset down again under debt FVOCI (unlike AMC)

**Gap:** Confused AMC-style asset write-down with FVOCI’s FV carrying amount; AOCI split was otherwise close.

## 2026-10-15 — Equity method: upstream/downstream unrealized profit

**Q1:** Upstream sale (BY/SY): SY sells goods costing 600 to BY (20% investor) for 1,000; BY still holds them at year end. How much does BY eliminate, and when does it come back?

**My answer:** Eliminate the full 400; it comes back when the goods are sold to a third party.

**Correct:**
- Eliminate only BY's share: $20\%\times400=\mathbf{80}$ (equivalently, adjust SY's NI by $-400$, then take 20%)
- Released when the goods are sold to an outside party (pro rata) ✓

**Gap:** Eliminated the full unrealized profit instead of the investor's % share.

**Q2:** Upstream sale of an asset the buyer uses as a fixed asset (DJ/EJ: profit 300, 10-year straight line). How does the unrealized profit come back?

**My answer:** Through amortization.

**Correct:** Through **depreciation** of the fixed asset: $300/10=30$ a year gross, investor share $20\%\times30=\mathbf{6}$ a year.

**Gap:** Wording: a fixed asset is depreciated, not amortized (amortization is for intangibles).

## 2026-10-17 — Goodwill, equity method, JVs and share-based pay

**Q1:** Full vs partial goodwill: if goodwill is fully impaired, which method shows the larger loss, and is NI the same under both?

**My answer:** NI is the same under both methods.

**Correct:**
- Full goodwill gives the larger impairment loss: **200** vs **180** under partial goodwill
- NI is the same under both only **before** any impairment

**Gap:** Missed that an impairment is larger under full goodwill.

**Q2:** Equity-method income for a 30% stake in an investee with NI of 300.

**My answer:** Deducted dividends from income.

**Correct:** Income $=30\%\times300=\mathbf{90}$. Dividends do **not** reduce income; they only reduce the carrying amount of the investment.

**Gap:** Netted dividends against equity-method income (second miss on this point).

**Q3:** Restating an equity-method JV with proportionate consolidation: what changes?

**My answer:** Thought NI or equity would change.

**Correct:**
- NI and equity: **unchanged**
- Revenue, assets and liabilities: **higher**; only the presentation changes

**Gap:** Proportionate consolidation changes presentation, not NI or equity.

**Q4:** Share-based pay with a market condition that is not met: what happens to the expense?

**My answer:** Reverse the expense.

**Correct:**
- Market condition missed: the expense **stays recognized** (no true-up)
- Service and non-market performance conditions: **trued up** to the awards that actually vest

**Gap:** Treated a market condition like a service or non-market condition.

**Q5:** Direction of the inputs to an employee stock option's fair value.

**My answer:** Mixed up the directions.

**Correct:**
- Higher risk-free rate, longer term, higher volatility: fair value **rises**
- Higher dividend yield: fair value **falls**

**Gap:** Option-pricing input directions; only dividends lower the value.

## 2026-10-19 — Share-based pay tax, anti-dilution, market conditions

**Q1b:** 4,000 ESOs at FV \$6 vest over 4 years (25% tax rate). After vesting, 3,000 are exercised at a market price of \$10, with an exercise price of \$2. What is the DTA at the end of vesting, and what is the tax deduction? (The scenario was corrected from a flawed original item.)

**My answer:** D: a 30,000 deduction and no DTA.

**Correct:** The DTA is 24,000 × 25% = **6,000** at the end of vesting. The deduction is the intrinsic value, 3,000 × (\$10 − \$2) = **24,000**.

**Gap:** Used market price × shares for the deduction, and missed that a DTA exists until exercise. It builds with the book expense during vesting.

**Q2b:** The deduction at settlement is 180,000: what is the excess tax benefit?

**Correct:** **7,500** excess tax benefit (IFRS: equity; US GAAP: lower tax expense).

**Gap:** Missed the windfall calculation.

**Q3b:** Exercise price \$40 vs average price \$35, basic EPS \$2.00: what is diluted EPS?

**Correct:** The options are anti-dilutive and excluded, so diluted EPS = **\$2.00**. Negative incremental shares are never added.

**Gap:** Counted out-of-the-money options.

**Q7b (repeat):** A market-condition award (16m of 24m expensed) misses its target.

**Correct:** The 16m **stays**, with no true-up, because the condition is in the grant-date fair value.

**Gap:** Second miss on market vs non-market conditions.

## 2026-10-20 — Goodwill, FX transactions, temporal method

**Q3:** Price 800 for 80%; FV of identifiable net assets 750; FV of NCI 190. Full vs partial goodwill?

**My answer:** 50.

**Correct:** Full = 800 + 190 − 750 = **240**; partial = 800 − 0.8 × 750 = **200**.

**Gap:** Wrong goodwill formula; full goodwill adds NCI fair value and subtracts 100% of net assets.

**Q4:** EUR import payable, and the EUR appreciates before settlement.

**My answer:** FX gain.

**Correct:** **FX loss** in NI; inventory stays at the transaction-date rate.

**Gap:** Reversed the direction for a foreign currency payable.

**Q5:** Temporal method, net monetary assets, foreign currency appreciates.

**My answer:** Remeasurement loss.

**Correct:** **Remeasurement gain** in NI; exposure is the net monetary position.

**Gap:** Reversed the sign of temporal-method exposure.
