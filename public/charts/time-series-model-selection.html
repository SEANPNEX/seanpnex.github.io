Why each branch exists

01 · Stationarity gate

Unit root supported → difference and retest. AR, MA, and ARMA assume a stationary modeled series. ARIMA is allowed when its d differences produce stationarity.

02 · Mean-model gate

Residual correlation → revise and refit. ARMA-family innovations should be white noise; remaining serial correlation means predictable mean structure was missed.

03 · Variance-model gate

ARCH effects → consider ARCH/GARCH. Model conditional variance only after the mean equation is adequate and squared residuals show volatility dependence.

Assumptions that admit each model

AR / MA / ARMA

Modeled series is covariance stationary; AR roots satisfy stationarity, MA side is invertible, and innovations are white noise.

ARIMA(p,d,q)

The level may be nonstationary, but differencing exactly d times must yield a stationary ARMA process. Residuals must then be white noise.

Trend / seasonal

Trend or calendar structure is deterministic and stable; after removing it, the remainder must satisfy the chosen error-model assumptions.

ARCH / GARCH

The conditional mean is adequate, variance remains positive, and squared residuals exhibit dependence or volatility clustering.

Cointegration

Nonstationary series share an integration order and a linear combination is stationary; use error correction for joint dynamics.

Failing to reject is not proof

Use DF or ADF terms consistent with the hypothesized deterministic structure, then retest after transformation.

Diagnostics outrank fit

Residual correlation means the mean specification remains incomplete, even when in-sample fit looks strong.

Parsimony survives validation

Prefer the smallest stable model whose residuals are adequate and whose unseen-observation error is credible.
